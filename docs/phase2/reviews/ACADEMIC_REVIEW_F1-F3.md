## 1. Verdict and executive summary

None of the three packages is defensible as written, and all three are close.
The captures are excellent: careful, cited to the line, and honest about their gaps.
The trouble sits in the README and ANALYSIS layers, where several sentences say more than the captures found.
**F1** is defensible as a concept section once the IDTx has one definition and the "no contract can read a log" claim is narrowed to direct reads.
**F2** is the soundest package: it mostly restates V rows, but two headline sentences ("gas transfers to the unit", "a cached history matches the database") contradict the evidence they cite.
**F3** is not defensible as written: its read-side headline forms ratios across three runs, miscounts the orders of magnitude, and makes a radio claim the testbed cannot support.
Its taxonomy is a good paper frame, and P2-D7 already gives the thesis the six-cell frame instead.
The prototype and the SQLite backend are sound instruments; each has one overclaim (AR-4, AR-22).
**The single most important fix:** adopt one IDTx definition with explicit exclusions (§3.3), then re-classify the 25 Phase 1 instances under it.
The concept carries the strongest later paper, and today an examiner can show that most of the cited instances are ordinary access control.

### 3.2 Related-work paragraph the author can adapt

> Contracts that gate execution on attributes are not new. Role and owner modifiers implement attribute-based access control in the sense of NIST SP 800-162. ERC-3643 gates token transfers on claims held in an ONCHAINID registry. ERC-4337 moves admission into programmable validation. Oracle systems such as Town Crier import off-chain facts to gate execution. Architecture work has also classified where blockchain applications keep their data, on chain or off it (Xu et al.; Eberhardt and Tai). This work does not claim the mechanism. It claims three things for vehicle identity. The first is a definition that separates a transaction depending on another identity's lifecycle state from administration of that identity's own record and from authentication of the sender. The second is a taxonomy that ties each dependency to the storage locus of the state it reads, and so to what that choice permits. The third is a freshness dimension, measured for messages in this thesis, that is absent from access-control and token-compliance work. The ordering race between a dependency's change and the dependent transaction belongs to the transaction-order-dependence literature (Luu et al.; Daian et al.). This work identifies that race and does not measure it.

### 3.3 The definition I recommend

> An **identity-dependent transaction (IDTx)** is a transaction whose admissibility or effect is a function of the state of an identity that the transaction names. That state is evaluated during execution, either from ledger state or from an attestation or proof that the transaction carries and verifies. Two cases are excluded: authentication of the transaction's own sender, and a check whose only subject is the identity record that the transaction itself modifies (identity administration). An off-chain decision that depends on identity state, such as a verifier accepting a message, is an **identity-dependent decision**. It shares the freshness dimension but is not a transaction.

Under this definition, `payToll` and the prototype's `fulfil` are IDTx, and `onlyOwner` on `setAttribute` is administration. The V2V and RSU checks (T19 to T22) are decisions.
