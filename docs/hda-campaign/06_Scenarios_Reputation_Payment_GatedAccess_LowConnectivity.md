## 1. Purpose and common frame

### 1.1 Claim

The four scenario families treated here — reputation, payment, gated access, and low connectivity — are the ones in which the identity system is most tempted to do something it should not: hold money, hold a location trail, hold a live access-control list, or demand a chain round-trip from a vehicle that has no link. This document argues, per pair, that a single placement discipline resolves all four temptations. The chain holds only what must be globally agreed and permanently attributable (identifiers, credential anchors, revocation state, endorsement state, presentation commitments, and batch roots); everything with volume, latency, or personal-data character stays off-chain and is bound to the chain by a hash; and every verifier keeps a cache whose staleness window is a declared security parameter rather than an accident. The placement analysis that justifies this discipline is in 01 and the synthesis design is in 03; this document instantiates them on four concrete pairs, each with a connected and autonomous vehicle (CAV) instance and an artificial-intelligence (AI) agent instance, and states the sandbox demonstration that would produce evidence for each.

### 1.2 Actors and objects shared by all four pairs

Every scenario draws on the same cast of decentralised identifiers (DIDs), each created with F1 and controlled by an externally owned account or, for agents, an account-abstraction principal (see 04 and the C14 wrapper). The roles are: a **vehicle DID** (the on-board unit (OBU) acts as an F11 `sigAuth` delegate of it, exactly as in scenario S1, where the delegate was granted for 3,600 s at 54,227 gas on O1); a **driver DID**; an **operator DID** for each roadside or commercial service (toll operator, charging network, depot, low-emission-zone (LEZ) authority, parking operator); a **roadside unit (RSU)** DID that is an F11 delegate of the operator that owns it; an **authority DID** (traffic authority, transport ministry) that acts as endorser (G2) and, for reputation, as attester (C1); an **agent DID** for the AI-agent instances, with a **principal DID** that delegated it; a **marketplace or tool-provider DID**; and an **oracle DID** for the price and settlement feeds, following the 2020 CVIN paper's distributed-oracle stance [5].

The objects are: verifiable credentials (VCs) issued with F5 and anchored as `credentialHash` plus `validUntil`; verifiable presentations (VPs) committed with F8 under a challenge; endorsements recorded with G1–G4 in the EndorsementRegistry named in the brief §7; reputation attestations recorded with C1 and read with C2; validation requests C3/C4 where an operator wants a third party to check an artefact; off-chain report bundles, receipts, access logs and event queues, each reduced to a Merkle root; and secure identity-to-identity (I2I) channels whose construction is 08's subject and is not repeated here.

### 1.3 Placement columns, tier, and failure taxonomy

Each datum in this document is assigned to one of four placement columns, which are the columns of 01's placement matrix and of the consolidated table in §6: **on-chain state** (a storage write that changes what F2, F6, F12, G4 or C2 return); **on-chain anchor** (a hash, root or commitment written so that an off-chain object becomes tamper-evident, typically via F5 or F8, or as event log data); **off-chain signed** (a document or message carrying an EIP-191 secp256k1 Data Integrity proof from the `2_w3c-ssi-layer` VC layer, verifiable with the DID document alone); and **cached** (a local copy of on-chain state with a recorded fetch height and a declared staleness window). The distinction between the first two columns is the distinction between what the chain *knows* and what the chain merely *witnesses*, and it drives cost: a state change costs a new storage slot (20,000 gas per fresh SSTORE, DIGEST-LEVEL, EIP-2929 figures), whereas a witness can be as cheap as event log bytes (8 gas per byte, DIGEST-LEVEL) or, on a rollup, blob space.

Latency tiers follow the C6 framework restated in the brief §2: Tier 1 (<100 ms) is out of scope for any distributed ledger; Tier 2 (100–500 ms) covers toll authorisation, charging initiation, parking and gated-area credential exchange and is the primary target; Tier 3 (>500 ms) covers reputation, settlement, dispute, and history. The consequence that recurs in every pair is that *no Tier 2 decision may wait for a chain write*; Tier 2 decisions are made from signatures, cached state and pre-issued credentials, and the chain is written afterwards, in batches, at Tier 3.

The failure modes examined per pair are drawn from the supervisor memos' threat model (brief §2): impersonation, replay or equivocation, key compromise, Sybil or collusion, credential theft or mis-issuance, supply-chain compromise, and chain faults (reorganisation, rollback, partition, delayed finality). Each pair names the two or three that are decisive for it; 08 treats the channel-level cruxes in depth.

### 1.4 The demonstration hook

Every scenario ends with a demonstration hook naming the sandbox script, the trace assertion, and the metric. The scripts proposed here (S11–S14) extend the catalog in `5_minimal-ssi-sandbox/docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §4 beyond S1–S6 and beyond the S7/S8 VID scripts named in the brief §7; they are proposals until built, and any gas figure attributed to them is a target. The only measured figures cited in this document are those in the S1 trace `traces/i2i_O1_ERC1056_1780620182135.jsonl`: F1 161,823–178,935; F5 130,163; F9 59,607; F11 54,227; F8 62,441; F7 32,980; F10 36,478; F3 44,760; F4 55,127; view functions F2, F6, F12 gas-free. Where this document multiplies or divides those figures it is doing arithmetic on measured values, and the result is an *estimate for O1 on a local Hardhat EVM*, not a measurement of the proposed scenario.

## 2. Reputation

### 2.1 Claim

Reputation is the one object in the identity system that is *inherently linkable*: a score is worthless unless it accrues to a stable identifier across many interactions. The design problem is therefore not how to make reputation private, which is impossible, but how to keep the linkable object (the score on a verinym) separate from the unlinkable objects (the individual reports and actions under pseudonyms) while still allowing the score to be earned by those actions. The dual-mode identity of the 2020 CVIN paper [5] resolves this exactly: reputation attaches to the verinym, evidence is produced under pairwise pseudonyms, and a zero-knowledge (ZK) link proof binds the two only at the moment of scoring. Document 09 gives the full argument for why federated learning and other collective computation need reputation, why reputation needs identity, and why identity needs this system; this section instantiates the mechanics and the placement.

### 2.2 CAV instance: road-condition reports scored by a traffic authority

**Actors.** Vehicle verinym DID *V* (the MOBI Vehicle Identity (VID) subject); a pool of pseudonymous DIDs *P₁…Pₖ* controlled by the same OBU, each created with F1 (a per-pseudonym cost of roughly 162K gas on O1 by the S1 measurement, which is why pseudonym pools are provisioned at Tier 3 and rotated on a schedule rather than per report); an RSU DID *R*, F11 delegate of the regional traffic authority DID *T*; the ministry DID *M* that endorses *T* (G2) as an attester for reputation of type `ROAD_REPORT_QUALITY`.

**Objects.** Off-chain road-condition reports (pothole, ice, obstruction, congestion) signed by a pseudonym; a periodic **report bundle** per pseudonym, reduced to a Merkle root; a reputation attestation (C1) on *V* with `evidenceURI` pointing to the bundle root; the ZK link proof that *Pᵢ* and *V* share a controller, presented as part of an F8 presentation.

**Function sequence.**

1. Tier 3, once: *M* → G1 `registerIssuer(T, ATTESTER)`; *M* → G2 `endorseIssuer(M, T, ROAD_REPORT_QUALITY, validity)`. *V*'s OBU → F1 ×k for the pseudonym pool; *V* → F11 `addDelegate(V, sigAuth, obu, validity)` so that the OBU signs on the verinym's behalf.
2. Tier 2, per report: the OBU signs a report under *Pᵢ* and sends it to *R* over the I2I channel of 08. *R* verifies the signature against *Pᵢ*'s cached DID document (F2, cached) and appends the report to its off-chain ingest. Nothing is written on-chain per report.
3. Tier 3, per epoch (for example, daily): *R* computes, per contributing pseudonym, a quality score by comparing the pseudonym's reports against ground truth (other reports, sensor fusion at the RSU, road-crew confirmation). The pseudonym's reports for the epoch are bundled and the Merkle root *ρᵢ* is computed.
4. Tier 3, epoch close: the OBU, wishing the score to accrue to *V*, produces an F8 presentation on *V* whose challenge is `keccak256(ρᵢ ‖ epoch ‖ T)` and attaches a ZK proof that the controller of *Pᵢ* is the controller of *V* (the proof construction is 02's subject; the sandbox-realisable form is a signature under both keys over the same challenge, which proves co-control but not in zero knowledge, and is labelled as such in the demonstration). *T* verifies the presentation (F8 event plus off-chain proof) and calls C1 `attestReputation(V, score, evidenceURI = "ipfs://…/" ‖ ρᵢ)`.
5. Tier 2, on the consuming side: the traffic model at *T* weights incoming reports from *Pⱼ* only after the OBU has shown, at channel setup, a threshold proof that C2 `getReputation(V)` exceeds *θ* — again through a link proof, so that *R* learns "this pseudonym belongs to a verinym with reputation above *θ*" and nothing else.

**Placement.** On-chain state: the DIDs, the endorsement of *T*, and the C1 attestation (score, attester, evidence URI). On-chain anchor: the F8 commitment binding *V* to *ρᵢ*. Off-chain signed: every report, every bundle, the RSU's scoring worksheet. Cached: DID documents of active pseudonyms at *R*; C2 result at *R* for the duration of a session.

**Cost consequence.** The per-report cost is zero on-chain; the per-epoch cost per contributing vehicle is one F8 (62,441 gas measured on O1 for a single-credential presentation) plus one C1 (unmeasured; O7 is not live in the sandbox, so the ERC-8004 reputation write is a target, and the ISetC interface makes the evidence URI a `string` in calldata, so its cost scales with URI length at 16 gas per non-zero byte, DIGEST-LEVEL). For a jurisdiction on the scale of Canada's roughly 33 million registered vehicles (DIGEST-LEVEL), this is the reason the epoch is a day and not an hour, and the reason 03 places the reputation registry on a Local CVIN sidechain with a periodic root anchored to the backbone rather than on the backbone itself.

**Sybil resistance.** Two mechanisms, both already in the design. First, reputation only accrues to a verinym that holds an endorsed VID: C1 is accepted by the registry policy only if F6 on *V*'s `VID_BIRTH` credential returns valid and G4 confirms the issuer was endorsed. A Sybil must therefore obtain a manufacturer-issued birth certificate, which is exactly the cost that Douceur's analysis says a Sybil defence must impose (NEW-6, DIGEST-LEVEL). Second, participation cost: each pseudonym costs an F1, and each epoch's accrual costs an F8. A flood of low-quality pseudonyms therefore costs gas without earning score, because the score is on the verinym and is a mean, not a sum.

**The unlinkability tension, stated honestly.** The link proof is where the design is clever and where it can break. If the proof is a co-signature (the sandbox-realisable form), *T* learns the *Pᵢ*↔*V* link, and *T* must be trusted not to publish it; the reports remain unlinkable to everyone else because only the root *ρᵢ*, not the pseudonym, appears in the attestation's evidence URI. If the proof is a genuine ZK membership proof (02, Semaphore-style, NEW-13 DIGEST-LEVEL), *T* learns only that some pseudonym in the epoch's contributor set is linked to *V*, and anonymity is bounded by the size of that set. What breaks the design is a small contributor set: a rural RSU with three contributing vehicles gives an anonymity set of three regardless of the proof system. The mitigation is aggregation across RSUs before scoring, which raises the set size at the cost of scoring latency, and 09 treats this trade-off as the reputation-privacy budget.

**Failure modes.** *Slander*: an RSU attests a low score without evidence. Mitigation: the attestation is attributable (C1 emits `ReputationAttested` with the attester), the evidence URI must resolve to a bundle whose root the OBU co-signed, and the vehicle can C3 `requestValidation(V, DISPUTE, payload)` naming the attestation; the ministry that endorsed *T* adjudicates and can G3 revoke the endorsement. *Collusion*: several vehicles corroborate false reports to raise each other's scores. Mitigation: ground-truth weighting at the RSU and anomaly screens (memo stance, brief §2), and the fact that the collusion is permanently visible in the bundles — the forensic-audit primitive of the Building Paths preface. *Key compromise of the OBU delegate*: reports are signed under compromised pseudonyms; the vehicle F10/F11 rotates, and the reputation on *V* survives because it was never bound to the compromised key.

**Problems solved and further functionality.** This is the road- and traffic-condition reporting case of the checklist item 9 directly: the reporting pipeline exists, scales because reports are off-chain, and is trustworthy because the reporter's history is on-chain. The same pipeline is the input to federated traffic-model training (09): a vehicle's model updates are "reports" of a different type, its reputation weights its updates in the aggregation, and a Byzantine-robust aggregator (Krum and its successors, NEW-9 DIGEST-LEVEL) becomes far cheaper when it may exclude updates from unendorsed or low-reputation contributors before running. Tolling and pay-as-you-use roads inherit the reputation channel as a dispute input: a vehicle whose reputation shows consistent honest reporting is a weaker candidate for toll-evasion investigation than one with none.

**Demonstration hook.** Proposed script S11 (§7.1): asserts that the trace contains no `kind=tx` entries between the report-ingest `kind=offchain` entries, exactly one F8 whose note carries the bundle root, one C1 whose `evidenceURI` contains the same root, and a C2 read whose mean reflects the attested score. Metric: on-chain gas per epoch per vehicle, and the ratio of `offchain` to `tx` entries.

### 2.3 Agent instance: marketplace agent with a reputation-above-threshold proof

**Actors.** Agent DID *A* (an ERC-4337-style account, C14), delegated by principal DID *O* through F11 with `delegateType = veriKey` for signing and a scoped `sigAuth` for acting; marketplace DID *K*; counterparty agents *B₁…Bₙ*; a validator DID *X* endorsed by *K* (G2) for `TASK_OUTCOME` validation; the ERC-8004-derived Set C registry (O7 pattern, [19]).

**Objects.** Task credentials (F5, issued by *K* to *A* on task assignment); outcome attestations (C1 by *X* or by the counterparty, with evidence URI to the signed execution trace); a validation request (C3) that *A* raises when it wants an independent check of its own output before it is scored; the threshold proof "C2(*A*).meanScore ≥ *θ* and attestationCount ≥ *n*" presented at session start.

**Function sequence.** Tier 3 setup: *K* → G1/G2 registers and endorses *X*; *O* → F11 `addDelegate(O, sigAuth, A_account, validity)` so that *A* acts as *O*'s delegate rather than as a free-floating principal, which is the confused-deputy guard used again in §3.3. Tier 2 per engagement: *B* → F12 `validDelegate(O, sigAuth, A_account)` (cached), *B* → C2 `getReputation(A)` (cached, gas-free), and *A* → F8 on the task credential with challenge bound to the session. Tier 3 per completion: *A* → C3 `requestValidation(A, TASK_OUTCOME, traceRoot)`; *X* evaluates off-chain and its answer is read by C4; then *B* or *X* → C1 `attestReputation(A, score, evidenceURI → traceRoot)`.

**Placement and cost.** As in the CAV case, execution traces are off-chain and only their root reaches the chain inside the attestation's evidence URI or the validation payload. The difference is that the agent's actions are *themselves* the evidence: the signed execution trace (memo stance: agents "leave signed execution traces") is the bundle. The agent instance therefore has an extra cost driver the CAV does not — the C3 payload is `bytes calldata` and its size is under the caller's control, so the design rule is that the payload is a root and a URI, never the trace.

**Attack surface and the on-chain/off-chain split of evidence.** Three attacks are specific to agent reputation. *Whitewashing*: an agent with a bad score abandons *A* and creates *A′*. Defence: reputation is meaningful only for agents whose principal *O* is itself endorsed (a legal-entity credential, 07), and *O*'s delegation to *A′* is visible in *O*'s DID document history (F11 events), so a verifier policy can require a minimum delegation age or can read the principal's aggregate reputation across delegates. This is where the on-chain state of delegation, which the CAV design uses for OBUs, earns its cost for agents. *Collusion rings*: *A* and *B* attest each other. Defence: attestations are weighted by the attester's own endorsement and reputation (EigenTrust-style transitive weighting, NEW-7 DIGEST-LEVEL), computed off-chain from on-chain events, so the registry stores raw attestations and the weighting is a verifier-side policy; this keeps C1 cheap and keeps the weighting revisable without a contract upgrade. *Slander* is as in the CAV case, with C3/C4 as the dispute channel.

**Decay and dispute.** ISetC exposes a mean and a count and no timestamp; decay is therefore an off-chain policy over the `ReputationAttested` event stream (block numbers give the timestamps at no extra cost). The design rule is that the registry never decays, because decay rules differ by marketplace; a verifier computes its own time-weighted score from events. Disputes use C3 with `validationType = DISPUTE`; the attestation itself is never deleted, because deletion would destroy the audit trail — a disputed attestation is answered by a second attestation from the adjudicator and by G3 against the attester if warranted.

**Problems solved and further functionality.** A federated-learning coordinator is a marketplace in this precise sense: it assigns tasks (rounds), receives outputs (updates), validates them (robust aggregation or held-out evaluation), and scores contributors. The agent instance is therefore the FL instance with the words changed, and 09 makes that identification the spine of its argument.

**Demonstration hook.** S11 agent half: assert that C2 before the engagement returns count 0 and after returns count 1 with the attested score; assert a C3 entry precedes the C1 entry; assert that the C3 payload length in the trace note is bounded (a root, 32 bytes, plus URI).

## 3. Payment

### 3.1 Claim

The identity system does not move money. It *authorises* a payment by proving that the payer holds the right credentials, and it *receipts* a payment by anchoring the outcome so that both sides hold tamper-evident proof. Everything between those two events — the transfer of value — belongs to a payment rail that is chosen independently: a token contract, a state channel, or a fiat system reached through an oracle. Keeping the split sharp is what allows the same identity layer to serve a toll gantry, a charger, and an API meter without becoming a payments company, and it is what keeps the vehicle's location trail off the ledger.

### 3.2 CAV instance: toll gantry and EV charging

**Actors.** Vehicle verinym *V* with OBU delegate; toll operator DID *Tₒ* and its gantry RSUs (F11 delegates); charging network DID *Cₙ* and its chargers; the oracle DID *Ω* for tariff and fiat settlement; the ministry *M* that endorses *Tₒ* for `TOLL_ACCOUNT` and *Cₙ* for `CHARGING_TARIFF` (G2); the driver DID *D* for driver-bound tariffs.

**Objects.** A `TOLL_ACCOUNT` credential (F5 by *Tₒ* to *V*: account reference, class, validity — content off-chain); a `CHARGING_TARIFF` credential (F5 by *Cₙ*); a **payment authorisation** (an F8 presentation on those credentials, challenge bound to gantry ID, timestamp bucket, and amount ceiling); a **payment execution** on the rail; a **receipt** (off-chain signed by the operator, containing the authorisation hash, the executed amount, and the rail reference); the **receipt anchor** (F5 by the operator to *V*, `credentialHash = keccak256(receipt)`, `validUntil = 0`, credential type `PAID_EVENT`); and a periodic **settlement root** anchored per operator.

**Function sequence.**

1. Tier 3, once: *M* → G1/G2 for *Tₒ*, *Cₙ*; *Tₒ* → F5 `TOLL_ACCOUNT` to *V*; *Cₙ* → F5 `CHARGING_TARIFF` to *V*.
2. Tier 2, at the gantry (the 100–500 ms budget of the C6 framework): the RSU issues a challenge; the OBU answers with an off-chain VP over `TOLL_ACCOUNT` signed under *V*'s OBU delegate key, plus a selective-disclosure proof (02) that discloses vehicle class and account validity and nothing else; the RSU verifies signature and disclosure locally, checks F6 and F12 from cache, checks G4 from cache, and authorises passage. **No on-chain write occurs within the Tier 2 window.** Whether the OBU also commits the presentation with F8 (62,441 gas measured on O1) is a policy choice: it produces an on-chain, replay-protected record of authorisation but at a per-passage cost that no toll network would accept on a backbone chain, so the design commits per passage only on a Local CVIN sidechain, or commits per epoch a root over all passages — the batched form of F8 discussed in 01.
3. Tier 2–3, execution: the rail moves value. Three rails are supported without changing the identity layer: (a) a token transfer from a vehicle-held balance to the operator, referenced by the authorisation hash; (b) a state channel between *V* and *Tₒ* opened at Tier 3 and updated per passage off-chain, settled on close (Lightning-style channels, NEW-11 UNVERIFIED); (c) fiat, where the oracle *Ω* attests off-chain that a card or account transaction cleared, and its attestation is what the receipt cites.
4. Tier 3, receipting: the operator signs a receipt and either anchors it individually (F5 `PAID_EVENT` to *V*, 130,163 gas measured on O1 for a single F5 — acceptable for a charging session, unacceptable per toll passage) or appends it to a per-epoch settlement bundle whose root is anchored once (one F5 per operator per epoch, the same 130,163 gas amortised over every passage in the epoch, which is arithmetic on a measured value and not a measurement of a batch). The vehicle's wallet stores the receipt and the Merkle inclusion proof against the anchored root.
5. Tier 3, dispute: either side presents the receipt, the inclusion proof, and the F5 anchor; the adjudicator checks F6 on the `PAID_EVENT` anchor and, if the operator's honesty is in question, G4 on the operator's endorsement.

**Placement.** On-chain state: `TOLL_ACCOUNT` and `CHARGING_TARIFF` anchors and their revocation status; operator endorsements. On-chain anchor: per-session `PAID_EVENT` (charging) or per-epoch settlement roots (tolling); optional F8 commitments. Off-chain signed: the VP, the receipt, the oracle's settlement attestation, the channel state updates. Cached: at every gantry, the F6/F12/G4 state for the operator's known fleet, refreshed on the revocation schedule of §4.

**Privacy: no location trail on-chain.** A per-passage on-chain record with the vehicle's DID would reconstruct every trip; this is the GDPR nuance of the Building Paths preface applied to tolling. Three layers prevent it. First, the anchored receipt hash is a commitment: the receipt contains the gantry ID and time, the chain sees only `keccak256(receipt)`, and without the receipt preimage (held only by *V* and *Tₒ*) the anchor reveals nothing. Second, settlement is batched: the epoch root is anchored by the operator with the operator as subject, not by or to the vehicle, so even the count of anchors per vehicle is hidden. Third, where a third party (an insurer, a pay-as-you-use road authority) needs to know that a vehicle paid, the vehicle presents a ZK proof of payment — inclusion of a receipt in an anchored root, satisfying a predicate such as "total paid in period ≥ amount due" — without revealing which gantries (02 §on inclusion proofs). What breaks this: an operator that anchors per passage with the vehicle as F5 subject leaks the trail through the `CredentialIssued` event's `subject` topic. The design rule is therefore that `PAID_EVENT` anchors to the vehicle are permitted only for events that are not location-sensitive (a charging session at a home charger, a monthly toll statement), and per-passage evidence lives only inside batched roots.

**Failure modes.** *Double-spend of an authorisation* (replay at a second gantry): the challenge binds gantry ID and time bucket, and if F8 is used, the registry's challenge-replay check rejects the repeat. *Operator equivocation* (two receipts for one authorisation): both hash to different anchors, both attributable to the operator's key, and the audit trail resolves it — the anchoring makes the fraud visible, it does not prevent it. *Chain reorganisation after anchoring*: a receipt whose anchor is reorganised out is still a signed off-chain document; the anchor is re-submitted, and the vehicle's wallet treats an anchor as final only after the finality depth 03 declares for the chosen chain. *Oracle compromise*: a false "cleared" attestation lets a vehicle pass without paying; mitigation is the oracle-set design of the 2020 paper (multiple oracles, threshold attestations) and the fact that fiat reversals surface at Tier 3 as disputes.

**Problems solved and further functionality.** This is toll payment and pay-as-you-use roads. Distance-based road pricing is the same design with the receipt content changed from "passage at gantry" to "kilometres in zone", produced by the OBU from its own odometry and signed, with the operator's countersignature after plausibility checks; the ZK proof of payment is what lets a jurisdiction verify road-use charges were paid without receiving trip data. Reputation (§2) enters as a dispute-weighting input, and federated learning enters because pricing models trained on demand data are themselves federated computations over exactly these off-chain bundles (09).

**Demonstration hook.** S12 (§7.2): assert that between the authorisation `offchain` entry and the passage decision there is no `tx`; assert that the receipt anchor `tx` (F5) carries `credentialHash` equal to the keccak of the receipt logged in the preceding `offchain` entry; assert that an F6 on the receipt anchor returns valid; assert for the tolling variant that N passages produce exactly one F5. Metric: on-chain gas per passage as gas(F5)/N with N passages per epoch.

### 3.3 Agent instance: budget-bounded authorisation, streaming micropayments, confused-deputy prevention

**Actors.** Agent *A* delegated by principal *O*; API provider DID *S* with a metering service; treasury contract or channel funded by *O*; the oracle *Ω* for fiat where applicable.

**Objects.** A `SPENDING_MANDATE` credential (F5 by *O* to *A*: budget ceiling, allowed counterparties, validity, content off-chain); a predicate proof "requested amount ≤ remaining budget" over the mandate and the agent's own spend ledger; a streaming channel between *A* and *S* with per-request off-chain state updates; the settlement root anchored on channel close; F11 delegate scope.

**Function sequence.** Tier 3 setup: *O* → F5 `SPENDING_MANDATE` to *A*; *O* → F11 `addDelegate(O, sigAuth, A_account, validity)` bounded to the mandate's validity; *A* opens a channel with *S* funded from *O*'s treasury (rail-specific). Tier 2 per request: *A* sends a signed channel update plus, at the start of each session, an F8-anchored or off-chain VP on the mandate with a predicate proof that the session's ceiling is within budget (02); *S* verifies from cache: F12 that *A* is still *O*'s delegate, F6 that the mandate is not revoked, and the predicate. Tier 3 on close: the final channel state is settled on the rail, and *S* → F5 `PAID_EVENT` to *A* (or to *O*) anchoring the settlement receipt; *O* can revoke the mandate with F7 (32,980 gas measured on O1) or the delegation with a new F11 of zero validity at any time.

**Confused-deputy prevention via delegate scope.** The confused-deputy problem here is an API provider, or a compromised tool, inducing *A* to spend *O*'s money on something *O* did not authorise. The identity layer's defence is structural: *A* never holds *O*'s keys, it holds a delegation with a `delegateType`, and the F11 types are the scope. The minimal interface defines `sigAuth` (may authenticate) and `veriKey` (may sign assertions); the design extends the type vocabulary beside the interface, without altering it, with typed delegations such as `spendAuth:<mandateId>` so that F12 `validDelegate(O, spendAuth:<mandateId>, A_account)` is the question every payee asks. A tool that induces *A* to act outside the mandate finds that *S′* cannot obtain a true F12 answer for its own mandate, and the audit trail shows *A*'s attempt. What breaks this: a payee that does not ask F12, or asks it against a stale cache after *O* has revoked. The staleness window of §4 and §5 is therefore also the payment-exposure window, and the budget ceiling in the mandate bounds the loss during it.

**Placement and cost.** The mandate is one F5 per agent per validity period; the delegation is one F11; per-request cost is zero on-chain; settlement is one rail transaction plus one F5 anchor per channel close. The cost driver is the number of channel closes, which is a treasury policy, not an identity-layer parameter.

**Problems solved and further functionality.** Pay-per-use of inference, data, or tool APIs by agents is pay-as-you-use roads with a different meter, and the same receipt-anchoring gives a principal an auditable spend history it can reconcile against the provider's claims. In a federated-learning marketplace the spending mandate is the coordinator's budget for contributor rewards and the receipts are the contributors' proof of payment (09).

**Demonstration hook.** S12 agent half: a mock rail (a minimal balance contract, deployed only for the scenario and not part of any option under evaluation) and a scripted over-budget request; assert that the predicate check logs `offchain` reject, that the F12 view for the wrong mandate type returns false, and that the settlement F5's hash matches the logged final channel state.

## 4. Gated access

### 4.1 Claim

Gated access is the case where the verifier is most often *disconnected* and the subject most often wants to be *unidentified*. Both requirements are met by a verifier that needs nothing from the chain at decision time: it holds the issuers' public keys (from cached DID documents), the issuers' endorsement status (cached G4), and a revocation snapshot (cached F6 or a status list), and it verifies a signature or a ZK predicate locally. The chain is consulted before the decision (to refresh the cache) and after it (to anchor the access log), never during it. The security parameter of such a gate is the age of its revocation snapshot, and the design's honesty consists in declaring that age rather than pretending the gate is online.

### 4.2 CAV instance: depot, restricted area, low-emission zone, parking

**Actors.** Vehicle *V*; zone authority DID *Z* (a municipality for the LEZ, a fleet operator for the depot, a parking operator for the garage); the gate controller as an F11 delegate of *Z*; the credential issuer *I* for the zone credential (for the LEZ, an emissions inspection body; for the depot, the fleet manager; for parking, the operator itself); the ministry or municipality *M* that endorses *I* for that credential type.

**Objects.** A zone credential (F5 by *I* to *V*: `LEZ_COMPLIANT` with emissions class, `DEPOT_ACCESS` with role, `PARKING_PERMIT` with zone and validity — content off-chain, using the `EmissionsCompliance` schema already in `2_w3c-ssi-layer`); the gate's cached issuer keys, endorsement snapshot and revocation snapshot with fetch height; the access-decision record; the per-gate access log and its epoch root.

**Function sequence.**

1. Tier 3, once: *M* → G1/G2 endorses *I* for the zone credential type; *I* → F5 to *V*.
2. Tier 3, on the gate's refresh schedule (every *Δ* minutes while connected): the gate fetches F2 for *I* and for *Z*, G4 for (*I*, type), and F6 for every credential it has seen or expects (or the status-list document 01 describes, whose root it verifies against the chain), and records the fetch height *h* and time *t₀*.
3. Tier 2, at the gate: the gate issues a challenge; the OBU answers with an off-chain VP. Two verification modes exist. *Signature-only*: the VP discloses the credential and the gate checks the issuer signature against the cached key, the cached endorsement, and the cached revocation status; the gate learns the vehicle's identity. *ZK predicate*: the OBU presents a proof that it holds a credential of the right type from an endorsed issuer, unrevoked at height *h*, satisfying the predicate (for example emissions class ≤ 2), and the gate learns nothing else — this is the LEZ and public-parking mode, because a municipality has no need to learn which vehicle entered, only that a compliant one did. 02 gives the proof constructions and their costs; the gate-side requirement is that verification be local and within the Tier 2 budget, which the DIGEST-LEVEL VehiclePassport figures (22 ms proving, sub-5 ms verification for vehicular attribute proofs, arXiv:2509.06133) suggest is realisable, though this campaign has not measured it.
4. Tier 2, decision: open if the checks pass and the snapshot age *t − t₀* ≤ *Δ_max*; if the snapshot is older than *Δ_max* the gate falls to the policy of §5 (fail-open for low-consequence zones such as parking, fail-closed for a secure depot).
5. Tier 3, logging: each decision (challenge, presentation hash, decision, snapshot height) is appended to the gate's signed log; at epoch close the gate anchors the log root — one F5 by *Z* to *Z* (subject = the zone's own DID, so no vehicle appears in the event topics), or one F8 commitment if the operator prefers the presentation-registry semantics. For the ZK mode the log holds nullifiers, not identities, and the anchor supports later audit of "how many distinct compliant vehicles entered" without deanonymisation.

**Endorsement check as the load-bearing step.** A forged credential from a fake issuer with a valid signature is the attack that a pure signature check misses; G4 `isEndorsed(I, type)` at the height of issuance is what closes it. The verification policy of the brief §7 — F6 valid *and* issuer endorsed for that type at issuance time — is enforced entirely from cache here, which is why endorsements must be cheap to snapshot: the EndorsementRegistry design in 03 keeps endorsement state small (one slot per (issuer, type) pair) precisely so that a gate can hold the whole endorsement table for its jurisdiction.

**Placement and cost.** On-chain state: credential anchors, revocation, endorsements. On-chain anchor: per-epoch log roots. Off-chain signed: the VP, the gate log. Cached: everything the gate needs to decide. Cost driver: the number of gates times the epoch frequency for anchors (one F5 each, 130,163 gas measured on O1 for a single F5, amortised over the epoch's decisions), plus the refresh traffic, which is read-only and gas-free (F2, F6, F12, G4 are views) but consumes RSU backhaul.

**Failure modes.** *Revoked credential still accepted*: within the snapshot age, by design; the exposure is bounded by *Δ_max* and by the zone's consequence. *Replay of a captured VP*: the challenge is gate-specific and time-bucketed, and the OBU signs it; a replayed VP fails the challenge check. *Key compromise of the gate delegate*: an attacker can forge log entries but not credentials; *Z* rotates the delegate with F11 and the log root anchored under the compromised key is disputed at Tier 3. *Partition between the gate and the chain*: the gate is designed for it; see §5.

**Problems solved and further functionality.** Gated access is the original medium-tier use case of the 2020 paper [5]. It extends directly to pay-as-you-use: a congestion-charge zone is a gate whose decision is "log a chargeable entry" rather than "open", and its anchored log root is the settlement input of §3. Road-condition reporting benefits because a gate is an RSU with a known location, and entries it logs are ground truth for the traffic model of §2. Federated learning of zone-demand models trains on the anonymised entry logs, and reputation of the reporting gates (an RSU is an identity with a score) weights their contributions (09).

**Demonstration hook.** S13 (§7.3): run the gate with a snapshot taken at height *h*; revoke the credential (F7) after the snapshot; assert that the gate's `offchain` decision entry still records `open` with `snapshotHeight = h`; refresh; assert the next decision records `deny`; assert that between the snapshot and the refresh the trace contains no `view` entries from the gate (proving that the gate did not touch the chain); assert one F5 log-root anchor at epoch close.

### 4.3 Agent instance: capability-gated tool and API access

**Actors.** Agent *A* delegated by principal *O*; tool or Model Context Protocol (MCP)-style server DID *S*; capability issuer *I* (the enterprise's platform team, or the tool vendor); the governance DID that endorses *I* for `TOOL_CAPABILITY`.

**Objects.** A capability credential (F5 by *I* to *A*: tool identifiers, permitted operations, rate ceilings, validity — content off-chain); the session-start VP; the server's locally enforced policy derived from the credential; the per-session access log and its root.

**Function sequence.** Tier 3: *I* → F5 to *A*; *O* → F11 delegation. Tier 2, session start: *A* → VP on the capability credential (F8 commitment optional; an enterprise audit policy may require it, at 62,441 gas measured on O1 per session, which is affordable at session granularity and not at call granularity); *S* verifies from cache F6, F12, G4 and derives a local policy object; every subsequent tool call is checked against that object with no identity-layer interaction. Tier 3, session end or epoch: *S* anchors its access-log root (F5, subject *S*).

**Revocation propagation latency as the security parameter.** An agent whose capability is revoked mid-session continues to act until *S* refreshes. The design makes this explicit: *S* declares its refresh interval *Δ* and its maximum session length *L*, and the exposure window is *Δ + L*. Three levers reduce it at different costs: shorter sessions (more VPs, each costing an F8 if committed); shorter *Δ* (more view traffic, gas-free but not free of infrastructure); or an event subscription, where *S* watches `CredentialRevokedEvent` and `DelegateAdded` on the registry and invalidates immediately — the cache-invalidation-synchronised-with-revocation pattern of the memos (brief §2) and the crux that 08 treats for I2I channels. The third is preferred where connectivity allows, and its failure mode is the partition case of §5.

**Placement and cost.** Identical to the CAV gate. The one agent-specific addition is that the local policy object is a derived artefact and must be regenerated, never edited, on every refresh; an edited policy is the agent-world equivalent of a gate with a hand-written allow list.

**Problems solved and further functionality.** Capability-gated tool access is the agent form of gated access, the A2A "session policy" phase of the memos, and the mechanism by which a federated-learning coordinator scopes what a contributor agent may read from and write to the shared model (09).

**Demonstration hook.** S13 agent half: assert that after F7 on the capability credential the server's next in-session call is still `offchain allow`, that the post-refresh call is `offchain deny`, and that the log-root anchor F5 follows.

## 5. Low connectivity

### 5.1 Claim

A vehicle in a tunnel and an edge agent on an intermittent backhaul face the same choice: act on stale state or do not act. The design answer is a declared connectivity mode with two sub-modes — **read-only** (act on cached state within a staleness window; refuse actions that need fresh state) and **delayed write** (sign events locally with monotonic counters, queue them, anchor in batch on reconnect) — and a reconciliation rule for the case that the world changed while the node was away. The safety argument is that no action taken stale can be worse than the action the node would take with no identity system at all, and that every stale action is signed and later anchored, so it is attributable even if it was wrong. Document 03 names the degraded modes as a design property of the hybrid; this section gives the state machine and the rules.

### 5.2 CAV instance: rural tunnel, remote highway

**Actors.** Vehicle *V* with OBU delegate; RSUs *R₁* (before the tunnel) and *R₂* (after); the operators whose credentials the OBU carries; the anchoring service *N* (either the OBU itself when it regains a link, or an RSU acting as a relay, or the Local CVIN sidechain node of the 2020 architecture).

**Objects.** The OBU's cache: DID documents of its own and its known counterparties, its credentials, their last-known F6 status, the endorsement table for its jurisdiction, the last block height *h₀* and time *t₀* at which it synchronised; the **event queue**: signed events (road-condition reports as in §2, toll passages as in §3, gate decisions as in §4, lifecycle events as in the S8 VID script) each carrying a monotonic counter *c* and a local timestamp *t_local*; the **batch anchor** on reconnect.

**Read-only mode: what is safe to take stale.** The OBU's rule set is a table, and it is the same table a verifier uses for a stale gate:

| Action class | Needs fresh state? | Stale policy | Justification |
|---|---|---|---|
| Verify a counterparty's signature against a cached DID document | No | Allow within *Δ_max* | Key rotation is rare; the risk is a rotated-away compromised key, bounded by *Δ_max* |
| Accept a counterparty's credential | No | Allow within *Δ_max*, flag as stale-verified | Revocation exposure bounded by *Δ_max*; same argument as §4 |
| Present own credential to a stale verifier | No | Allow | The verifier bears the staleness risk, and it is the verifier's *Δ_max* that governs |
| Open an I2I channel (08) | No, if both DID documents cached | Allow, with session length ≤ *Δ_max* − age | Channel keys are ephemeral; the DID document is only used for authentication |
| Issue or accept a payment authorisation (§3) | No | Allow up to the mandate ceiling | Loss bounded by the ceiling; receipt is queued |
| Accrue reputation (§2 step 4) | Yes | Refuse, queue the bundle root | The attestation is Tier 3 anyway |
| Accept a fresh credential from an issuer whose endorsement is not cached | Yes | Refuse | Endorsement is the load-bearing check of §4 and cannot be assumed |
| Any F1, F3, F4, F7, F9, F10, F11 on own DID | Yes | Queue (delayed write) | Key material changes must be globally ordered |
| Act on a counterparty whose cached status is `revoked` or `deactivated` | — | Refuse | Negative state never goes stale in the unsafe direction |

The last row carries the safety argument's asymmetry: cached *negative* state (revoked, deactivated, expired by `validUntil` against local time) is always honoured, because a revocation cannot be un-done (F7 is irreversible, F4 is irreversible), so a stale "revoked" is always still true. Only cached *positive* state can be wrong, and only in the direction of accepting something that has since been revoked, which the staleness window bounds.

**Delayed write.** Every event the OBU would have anchored while online is instead signed under the OBU's delegate key with (*V*, *c*, *t_local*, *h₀*, payload hash) and appended to the queue; *c* is a strictly increasing counter persisted in the OBU's secure element, and *h₀* is the last synchronised height, so that every queued event carries a witness of what the OBU knew. On reconnect the OBU (or *N* on its behalf) builds a Merkle tree over the queue and anchors the root with one F5 (issuer *V*, subject *V*, `credentialHash = root`, `validUntil = 0`, type `EVENT_BATCH`); events that individually require on-chain state changes (a queued F11 rotation, for example) are then submitted as their own transactions in counter order, after the batch root, so that the chain's ordering of them is provably consistent with the OBU's local ordering.

**Ordering and conflict rules.** (i) Within one OBU, counter order is authoritative; the anchoring service rejects a batch with a gap or a repeat in *c*. (ii) Across OBUs, local timestamps are advisory and the anchor's block height is authoritative; two vehicles' reports about the same pothole are ordered by their batch anchors' heights, not by *t_local*, and the traffic model of §2 treats *t_local* as a reported attribute. (iii) A queued event that conflicts with on-chain state at reconnect (a queued key rotation for a key that the chain shows was already removed by a recovery action from another controller) is *not* applied; it is still included in the batch root, because the fact that the OBU attempted it is evidence, and it is marked `superseded` in the OBU's own log.

**The reconciliation rule for a revocation that happened meanwhile.** Suppose credential *K* (the OBU's own, or a counterparty's that it accepted) was revoked at height *h_rev* with *h₀* < *h_rev* < *h_reconnect*. Events in the queue that relied on *K* fall into two classes by local time. Those with *t_local* − *t₀* ≤ *Δ_max* were taken *within policy*: the OBU was entitled to act stale, and the events are anchored as `stale-valid`. Those with *t_local* − *t₀* > *Δ_max* were taken *outside policy*: a well-behaved OBU should have refused them, and they are anchored as `stale-overrun` — anchored nonetheless, because the point of the audit trail is that the overrun is visible. In both classes the OBU, on learning of the revocation, must (a) stop relying on *K* immediately, (b) include in the batch a signed `revocation-observed` event with *h_rev* so that its subsequent behaviour is attributable, and (c) for its *own* revoked credential, refrain from presenting it again. What the rule does *not* do is pretend the stale actions did not happen or roll them back; a toll passage that occurred under a since-revoked account is settled at Tier 3 as a dispute (§3), not unwound on-chain. What makes the rule work is that the OBU's local clock is trustworthy enough to bound *t_local* − *t₀*; what breaks it is a manipulated clock, which is why the counter *c* and the secure-element persistence, not the clock, are the integrity anchors, and why a batch whose *t_local* values are non-monotonic in *c* is rejected.

**Connectivity state machine.** States: ONLINE, STALE-OK, STALE-EXPIRED, RECONCILING. Variables: age = *t_now* − *t₀*; *Δ_max* the declared staleness window; queue *Q*; link status.

| From | Event / guard | To | Actions |
|---|---|---|---|
| ONLINE | link lost | STALE-OK | freeze *t₀*, *h₀*; begin queueing all writes |
| ONLINE | write requested | ONLINE | submit tx directly; log `tx` |
| STALE-OK | age ≤ *Δ_max* and read requested | STALE-OK | serve from cache; tag result `stale(age)` |
| STALE-OK | write requested | STALE-OK | sign, increment *c*, append to *Q*; log `offchain queued` |
| STALE-OK | age > *Δ_max* | STALE-EXPIRED | refuse positive-state reads except the read-only table's "Allow" rows for which the action's own consequence is bounded; continue queueing with `stale-overrun` tag |
| STALE-OK or STALE-EXPIRED | link restored | RECONCILING | fetch heights *h₀*..*h_now*: F6 on every relied-upon credential, F2 on every relied-upon DID, G4 on every relied-upon issuer; classify *Q* |
| RECONCILING | classification done | RECONCILING | anchor root of *Q* (F5 `EVENT_BATCH`); submit state-changing events in *c* order, skipping `superseded` |
| RECONCILING | all submitted and final | ONLINE | reset *t₀*, *h₀*; clear *Q* (retain local log) |
| RECONCILING | link lost again | STALE-OK | keep partial progress; unanchored suffix of *Q* remains queued; counter continues |

The machine is deliberately not a specification of the sandbox; it is the design the sandbox script S14 mocks (§7.4). The "link lost again" row is the one that most implementations get wrong, and it is why the batch is a Merkle root over a *prefix* of the queue, so that a second batch can anchor the suffix without re-anchoring or reordering.

**Placement and cost.** On-chain state: nothing new. On-chain anchor: one F5 per reconnect (130,163 gas measured on O1 for a single F5, amortised over |*Q*|) plus one transaction per queued state-changing event. Off-chain signed: every queued event. Cached: everything the OBU acted on, with *h₀*. The cost driver is the number of reconnects, not the number of events, which is the property that makes delayed write scale: a vehicle that spends an hour in a tunnel produces one anchor.

**Failure modes.** *Chain fault at reconnect (partition, delayed finality)*: the OBU treats its anchor as final only at the declared finality depth and remains in RECONCILING; a reorganised-out anchor is resubmitted. *OBU compromise during the offline window*: forged events are signed by the compromised delegate; on reconnect the batch is anchored and, once the compromise is discovered, the vehicle F10/F11 rotates and disputes the batch; the batch root's `h₀` and counter range make the window of forged events precisely delimitable. *Relay RSU misbehaviour* (when *N* is an RSU): *N* can delay or drop the batch but cannot alter it, because the root is signed by the OBU; the OBU retries with another relay and the duplicate anchor is harmless (same root, idempotent by policy).

**Problems solved and further functionality.** Rural road-condition reporting is the motivating case: the roads with the worst connectivity are the ones whose condition is least reported, and delayed write is what makes a vehicle's tunnel or mountain-pass observations reach the traffic model at all. Tolling on remote highways is the same: a gantry with no backhaul authorises from cache and settles when it can. Federated-learning contributions from vehicles with intermittent links follow the same queue — model updates are queued events — and 09 discusses how the coordinator weights late-arriving updates from reputable but poorly connected contributors.

### 5.3 Agent instance: edge agent with intermittent backhaul and a lease credential

**Actors.** Edge agent *A* (an inference or control agent at a plant, a farm, a ship, or a vehicle fleet depot) delegated by principal *O*; the backhaul-side coordinator *K*; the lessor DID *L* (usually *O* itself, sometimes *K*) that issues the lease.

**Objects.** A `LEASE` credential (F5 by *L* to *A*: scope of autonomous action, ceilings, and — the essential field — an expiry `validUntil` short enough to bound autonomous action); the same cache, queue, and batch anchor as the OBU; the same state machine.

**The lease as the bound on autonomy.** Where the OBU's staleness window *Δ_max* is a policy the OBU enforces on itself, the agent's bound is a credential the agent *holds* and every counterparty checks. The lease is issued with `validUntil = t_issue + L`; F6 returns `expired = true` after that height regardless of connectivity, and the agent's own local check of `validUntil` against its clock enforces the same bound offline, because expiry — like revocation — is negative state that never goes stale in the unsafe direction. Renewal requires a fresh F5 (a chain write, 130,163 gas measured on O1 per lease), which is exactly the point: an agent cannot extend its own autonomy without the backhaul. The design lets *L* choose *L* per agent by risk: a control agent that moves physical equipment gets minutes; an inference agent that only reads gets days. The lease composes with the spending mandate of §3.3 and the capability credential of §4.3; all three are F5 credentials with `validUntil`, and all three expire the same way, so an agent's autonomous envelope is the intersection of three windows, each set by a different party.

**Function sequence and reconciliation.** As in §5.2, with two agent-specific rules. First, the queue holds *decisions* (signed records of what the agent did and why, with the model version and inputs' hash), which is what makes an edge agent's offline behaviour auditable in the operational-transparency sense the Building Paths preface gives for agents. Second, on reconnect the agent submits the batch root *before* requesting lease renewal, so that *L* renews only having seen what the agent did — the renewal is a reputation decision (§2.3) and the batch root is its evidence URI.

**Placement and cost.** The lease is one F5 per renewal, the batch is one F5 per reconnect; per-decision cost is zero on-chain. The cost driver is *L*'s renewal interval, which is the direct price of a tighter autonomy bound.

**Failure modes.** *Lease expires mid-task while offline*: the agent must stop, which is the intended behaviour and must be designed for at the task level (checkpointing); a task that cannot safely stop is a task that should not be delegated to an offline agent with that lease length. *Lessor compromised*: over-long leases are issued; the lessor's endorsement (G4) for `LEASE` issuance is what a counterparty checks, and the endorser revokes it (G3).

**Problems solved and further functionality.** Edge federated learning with intermittent participants is this scenario: the lease bounds how long a contributor may train on a stale global model before it must resynchronise, and the batch anchor is the contributor's proof of participation for reward (09).

**Demonstration hook.** S14 (§7.4), shared with the CAV half.

Two patterns are visible in the table. First, the "on-chain state" column is nearly constant across the eight rows — DIDs, credential anchors, revocation, endorsement, delegation — and is precisely the IMinimalSSI plus governance surface; nothing in these four families required a new kind of on-chain state, which is the empirical form of the minimality claim (C2, C3) and is the point 11 makes academically. Second, every cost driver is an *epoch or session count*, never an event count; that is the defining property of the anchor/cache/attest pattern in 03, and it is what makes the design's cost a policy choice rather than a scaling limit.

*Requires* a Set C implementation. ### 7.2 S12 — payment receipt (`scenarios/run_payment_receipt.js`, proposed)

**Steps:** F1 for *M*, *Tₒ*, *Cₙ*, *V*, *Ω*; G1/G2; F5 `TOLL_ACCOUNT` and `CHARGING_TARIFF` to *V*; F11 OBU delegate; a mock rail contract (`MockRail.sol`, scenario-only, not an option) with a balance for *V*; N = 10 gantry passages, each an `offchain` challenge, `offchain` VP with hash, `offchain` local verify (F6/F12/G4 reads from a cache object populated once before the loop, with the cache's fetch logged as `view` entries *before* the loop), rail transfer as a `tx` against `MockRail` (tagged `fnId = RAIL`, excluded from function coverage), and an `offchain` receipt with hash; then one F5 `PAID_EVENT` by *Tₒ* with subject *Tₒ* and hash = Merkle root of the ten receipts; one charging session with an individual F5 `PAID_EVENT` to *V*; an F6 on each anchor; a replayed VP that the challenge check rejects (`offchain reject`); if the option supports it, an F8 replay attempt that reverts, logged with the error name. Agent half: `SPENDING_MANDATE` F5, scoped F11, five metered requests as channel-state `offchain` entries, an over-budget request rejected by the predicate, an F12 with the wrong delegate type returning false, one settlement F5. **Trace assertions:** (a) no identity-layer `tx` or `view` inside the passage loop (rail transfers excepted); (b) exactly one F5 for ten passages, and its `credentialHash` matches the root logged in the preceding `offchain` entry; (c) F6 on both anchors returns valid; (d) the charging anchor's `subject` is *V* and the tolling anchor's subject is *Tₒ*, verified by parsing the `CredentialIssued` event; (e) the replay is rejected. **Metric:** gas(F5)/N as on-chain gas per passage; the F8 replay revert as a correctness result.

### 7.3 S13 — gated access, offline verifier (`scenarios/run_gated_access_offline.js`, proposed)

**Steps:** F1 for *M*, *I*, *Z*, *V*; G1/G2 for *I*; F5 `LEZ_COMPLIANT` to *V*; F11 gate delegate on *Z*; **snapshot**: the gate reads F2(*I*), G4(*I*, type), F6(credential) and logs them as `view` entries with `note = "snapshot h=<blockNumber>"`; decision 1: `offchain` challenge, VP, local verify, `open` with the snapshot height; F7 by *I*; decision 2 (still on the stale snapshot): `offchain open, stale`; refresh: `view` entries with the new height; decision 3: `offchain deny, revoked`; a forged credential from an unendorsed issuer *I′* (F1, F5 by *I′*), decision 4: `offchain deny, issuer-not-endorsed` with G4 false; epoch close: F5 log-root by *Z* to *Z*. Optional ZK-mode leg: replaced by a selective-disclosure VP from the `2_w3c-ssi-layer` SD-JWT-style layer, with the decision note recording which claims were disclosed. Agent half: capability credential, session-start VP, F7 mid-session, one in-session `allow`, post-refresh `deny`, log-root F5. **Trace assertions:** (a) between the snapshot `view` block and the refresh `view` block there are no `view` or `tx` entries attributed to the gate identity; (b) decision 2 is `open` and decision 3 is `deny`, with the F7 `tx` between them; (c) decision 4's G4 read is false; (d) the log-root F5's subject equals *Z*. **Metric:** the number of gate-attributed chain reads per decision (target zero), and the F7-to-deny latency in trace steps as the sandbox's expression of revocation-propagation latency (the wall-clock `ts` difference is reportable but is a Hardhat artefact, not a network measurement).

### 7.4 S14 — delayed-write reconciliation (`scenarios/run_delayed_write.js`, proposed)

**The mock provider.** Connectivity loss is simulated by wrapping the ethers signer used by the OBU identity in a proxy whose `sendTransaction` throws a synthetic `LINK_DOWN` error for the next *N* calls (or until an explicit `reconnect()`), while leaving `call` (views) either functional-from-cache or also throwing, depending on the sub-mode under test. The wrapper is scenario code, not sandbox infrastructure, and it must log every rejected send as `offchain queued` with the counter *c* and the local timestamp, so that the trace itself shows the outage.

**Steps:** F1 for *V*, *R₁*, *R₂*, an issuer *I*, and *M*; G1/G2; F5 credential *K* to *V*; F11 OBU delegate; sync: `view` entries F2/F6/G4 with `note = "sync h0=<height>"`; **link down** (`offchain state=STALE-OK`); five queued events (road reports and one toll passage) as `offchain queued c=1..5`; a queued state-changing event (F9 add key) as `offchain queued c=6 kind=F9`; meanwhile, from another signer that is *not* wrapped, *I* → F7 revokes *K* (`tx`, `identity = Issuer`); two more queued events relying on *K* (`c=7, 8`) with *t_local* within *Δ_max*, and one (`c=9`) with *t_local* advanced past *Δ_max* by the script (`offchain queued stale-overrun`); a refused action: a fresh credential from an unendorsed issuer, `offchain refused endorsement-not-cached`; **reconnect** (`offchain state=RECONCILING`); reconciliation reads: F6(*K*) returns revoked, logged `view`, followed by `offchain revocation-observed h_rev=<height>`; classification: `offchain classify c=7,8 stale-valid; c=9 stale-overrun`; batch anchor: one F5 `EVENT_BATCH` with hash = root over *c*=1..9 plus the revocation-observed event; then the queued F9 as its own `tx`; then `offchain state=ONLINE`. A second leg tests "link lost again" mid-reconciliation: the proxy re-arms after the batch anchor and before the F9, and the assertion is that the F9 is re-queued with its original *c* and anchored in a second, disjoint batch. Agent half: identical, plus a `LEASE` F5 with `validUntil` = current time + short window at the start, the script advancing Hardhat's block time past it during the outage, and the reconciliation reads showing F6 `expired = true`, followed by the batch anchor and then a renewal F5 by *L*.

**Trace assertions:** (a) after the `state=STALE-OK` entry and before `state=RECONCILING` there are exactly *N* `offchain queued` entries from the OBU identity and zero `tx` entries from it, while there *is* one `tx` (the F7) from the issuer identity — the trace shows the world changing while the OBU is away; (b) queued counters are strictly increasing without gaps; (c) the batch anchor `tx` has `fnId = F5`, its note carries the root, and it precedes any queued state-changing `tx`; (d) the `revocation-observed` entry's height is greater than the sync height and less than the reconnect height; (e) exactly one event is classified `stale-overrun`; (f) in the second leg, the two batch roots are different and the F9 `tx` appears exactly once, after the second root; (g) agent half: the F6 read at reconciliation shows `expired = true`, and the renewal F5 follows the batch anchor. **Metric:** on-chain gas per queued event as gas(batch F5)/|*Q*|, and the number of chain transactions per reconnect (target: one plus the number of queued state changes).

### 7.5 What the traces will and will not show

The four scripts share a trace-schema convention: `kind=offchain` entries with a `note` beginning `queued`, `snapshot`, `state=`, or `classify` are the evidence that the hybrid is doing what the design says, and `kind=tx` entries are the evidence of what it cost. The schema in `traces/SCHEMA.md` accommodates all of this without change; the only extension proposed is the use of `fnId` values `C1`–`C4`, `G1`–`G4`, and the scenario-only tag `RAIL`, which the S1 coverage summary should exclude from its 12-function count. What the traces will *not* show is any network property — latency, revocation-propagation time, backhaul cost — because Hardhat is a local EVM; the trace step ordering is the sandbox's proxy for ordering-dependent security claims, and any timing claim from these scripts must be labelled a Hardhat artefact. Realising the latency claims of §4 and §5 requires the edge deployment 05 discusses, which is outside this pass.

## 10. Open questions for the researcher

1. **Link-proof form for the demonstration.** S11 as proposed uses a co-signature (non-ZK) link proof because it is sandbox-realisable today. Should the campaign accept a non-ZK demonstration of the dual-mode reputation pattern in 10, with the ZK form deferred to the ZKP angle pass, or should S11 wait for a Semaphore-style circuit and a Groth16 verifier (DIGEST-LEVEL 200–350K gas per on-chain verification) so that the first measurement is of the real thing? This decides whether Chapter 4 can cite a reputation trace in this pass.

2. **Set C host.** S11's C1–C4 need an ISetC implementation. Building O7 (ERC-8004 pattern) properly is a work order of its own under the sandbox's native-pattern rule; a `SetC_Reference.sol` scaffold would produce Set C gas figures faster but not for a real option. Which is acceptable evidence for the thesis: reference-scaffold gas labelled as such, or nothing until O7?

3. **Typed delegation vocabulary.** §3.3 proposes `spendAuth:<mandateId>`-style F11 delegate types beside the interface. The minimal spec names only `sigAuth` and `veriKey`. Is extending the *type vocabulary* (not the signature) consistent with the "interface is normative" rule as you intend it, or must scoped authorisation live entirely in the credential layer with F11 kept to the two spec types?

4. **Anchoring granularity policy for location-sensitive events.** §3.2 rules that per-passage anchors with the vehicle as F5 subject are forbidden because the `CredentialIssued` subject topic leaks the trail. This forbids a pattern the S3 catalog entry ("payment-anchored receipt") may have intended. Should S3 be revised to the batched-root form, and should the rule be stated as a design invariant in 03?

5. **Staleness window ownership.** §4 and §5 make *Δ_max* a declared parameter but do not say who declares it: the verifier (gate), the issuer (in the credential), or the jurisdiction (Local CVIN policy). Each choice changes who bears the exposure. Which is the thesis position, and should it differ between CAV (jurisdictional) and agent (principal-declared) instances?

6. **Whether stale-overrun events are anchored.** §5.2 anchors out-of-policy events on the argument that visibility beats suppression. A regulator might prefer that a compliant OBU be *unable* to act past *Δ_max* rather than able-but-recorded. Does the thesis take the audit-trail position unconditionally, or does it distinguish safety-critical action classes where the OBU must hard-refuse?
