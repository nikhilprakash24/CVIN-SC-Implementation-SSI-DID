ERC-4337 — Account-Abstraction Identity

EIP-4337 · smart account + EntryPoint · guardian recovery

Identity as an account-abstraction smart wallet: the account address is the stable vehicle identifier, the signing key can rotate, and a social guardian can recover control. Measured with a minimal representative EntryPoint, so its gas is a lower bound.

What it is
Account abstraction: the vehicle identity is a smart-contract account whose address never changes even as its signing key rotates, and a designated guardian can install a new key (social recovery). Operations can be routed as PackedUserOperations through an EntryPoint that validates the signature before executing.
Reference
eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-4337
Role in thesis
The account-abstraction + guardian-recovery substrate; one of the nine compared standards and the one exercising key-rotation and social-recovery identity semantics.
Where used
1_blockchain-identity/contracts/ERC4337/CVINVehicleAccount.sol, 1_blockchain-identity/contracts/ERC4337/CVINMinimalEntryPoint.sol
Provenance
Standard selection and comparative design are the researcher's (SOURCES.md §1). This is a minimal representative implementation: CVINMinimalEntryPoint is a research harness, not the canonical v0.7 EntryPoint — no bundler batching, paymasters, deposits or gas accounting. That honest scoping is a researcher-directed decision; the reported gas is therefore a lower bound (PROVENANCE.md).
Lower bound

Creating an identity costs 768,204 gas (direct account deployment), and routing an operation through the EntryPoint adds +46,862 gas of indirection over a direct call. Because this is a minimal representative EntryPoint with no bundler/paymaster overhead, these figures are a lower bound — real-world 4337 costs more. (Results §5.2.)
