# erc-735 — ERC-735 claim holder (plan S7 demos)

**Identity model.** One `CVINVehicleClaimHolder` contract per vehicle: deployment with the VIN is creation, the contract address is the identifier, `vin`/`vinHash` are immutable, and a single `owner` plays the ERC-734 MANAGEMENT key. Trusted issuers sign attestations off-chain; the owner anchors them as claims whose issuer signature is verified **on-chain at add time** (EIP-191 ECDSA), so later verifiers read `getClaim`/`claimExists` in O(1). Since D25 (`docs/DEFECT_LOG.md`, fixed 2026-10-04) the owner also keeps a per-topic **issuer registry** (`authorizeIssuer` / `revokeIssuer` / `isAuthorizedIssuer`) that `addClaim` enforces — self-issued claims (issuer == owner) are exempt — and a `VIN_ATTESTATION` claim must encode this holder's VIN.

Run: `cd 1_blockchain-identity && npx hardhat run ../sandbox/options/erc-735/demos/<family>.js`.

## Asymmetry notes

- **Creation: deployed**, VIN fixed in the constructor (≈1.60M after D25; ≈1.37M in the L1 benchmark, pre-registry). No cross-identity registry: VIN uniqueness across identities is unenforceable (D25b binds a topic-1 claim to *its* holder's VIN, not the VIN to one holder); `vinHash` is the indexed key for an off-chain VIN→contract index. The issuer registry starts empty, so every third-party claim costs one extra `authorizeIssuer` (48,317 gas, cold SSTORE) per (issuer, topic) first.
- **Veracity / automation.** Chain enforces: owner-only anchoring (holder consent — even an authorised issuer cannot push a claim), **issuer signature validity** (ecrecover, EIP-2 low-s, 65 bytes, scheme 1), claim binding to (this contract, topic, data), **issuer admission per topic** (D25a: `isAuthorizedIssuer(issuer, topic)` or issuer == owner), **VIN binding of topic-1 claims** (D25b: the trailing `bytes(vin).length` bytes of `data` must hash to `vinHash`), per-claim removal rights (owner or that issuer). It does **not** enforce: that a registered issuer is a genuinely accredited authority (the owner decides whom to admit; there is no global OEM/inspector registry), currency (no expiry), identity-level status (no revoke, no renounce, no freeze). Issuer revocation is forward-looking: existing claims survive `revokeIssuer` until `removeClaim`.
- **Cryptography / hashing.** `claimId = keccak256(issuer ‖ topic)` (one claim per issuer per topic); digest `keccak256(abi.encodePacked(identity, topic, data))` signed **with the EIP-191 `"\x19Ethereum Signed Message:\n32"` prefix** (personal_sign-compatible) — incompatible with CVIN-Combined's raw-digest convention (shown: a raw signature is rejected here). **D25c is recorded as a design decision for the author, not fixed:** `_recoverSigner` documents the asymmetry and AFTER_ACTION_REPORT_05 §1 F7 recommends EIP-191 as the single scheme; `claims.js` keeps the raw-digest negative and a `signature-scheme-D25c` note. `vinHash = keccak256(bytes(vin))`; ERC-1271 contract issuers unsupported.
- **Implemented but not compared.** `ClaimChanged` in-place updates, issuer-side revocation, `claimExists`, `getClaimIdsByTopic`, the INSPECTION/INSURANCE topics, the malleability guard, `ClaimRequested` compatibility events, and the whole D25a registry (`authorizeIssuer` 48,317 / `revokeIssuer` 26,420 gas, `IssuerAuthorized`/`IssuerRevoked` events, authorisations surviving `transferOwnership`).
- **Observed surprises.** *Fixed 2026-10-04 — D25a/D25b (`docs/DEFECT_LOG.md`), the demos assert the reverts and mark the steps `FIXED (D25a)` / `FIXED (D25b)`:* two competing manufacturer certificates can no longer coexist (an impostor's `MANUFACTURER_CERT` reverts with `issuer not authorized for topic`; the verifier no longer picks by address), and a topic-1 attestation for a different VIN, a truncated VIN or a VIN followed by further bytes reverts with `VIN attestation does not match holder VIN`. *Still true:* removed claims leave no tombstone (zeros = never issued); `getClaimIdsByTopic` order changes on removal; the identity can never be made ownerless or revoked; `isAuthorizedIssuer(owner, topic)` is `false` although the owner may self-issue (the exemption is not reflected in the registry); the adapter's default issuer (`deployer`) must be authorised by the caller before `adapter.addClaim` succeeds.
